# Content pillars

Generic marketing splits content 40 educational / 25 thought leadership /
25 social proof / 10 promotional. That mix is built for SaaS founders and it is
wrong for this business.

Shawn's credibility comes from having been on the roof. The mix reflects that.

| Pillar | Share | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Field proof | 40% | A real job. What was found, what it meant, what got done. |
| Plain-English code | 30% | Translating a requirement a customer actually asked about. |
| How the work works | 20% | Demystifying inspection, deficiency, repair, and the paperwork. |
| Ask | 10% | A direct offer. Rare, specific, and never dressed up. |

## Field proof (40%)

The backbone. One job, one finding, one outcome.

Shape: what the building was, what was found, what it would have caused, what
happened next. Four beats, short.

Strongest version is a near miss that got caught. Second strongest is a finding
that looked minor and was not.

## Plain-English code (30%)

Take a requirement a customer got cited on or asked about, and explain it the way
Shawn would explain it standing in the mechanical room.

Anchor to the standard: NFPA 10 for extinguishers, NFPA 25 for water-based
systems, NFPA 72 for alarm. Give the frequency and the reason for the frequency.
The reason is the content. Anyone can quote a table.

## How the work works (20%)

Property managers do not know what happens during an inspection, why a deficiency
report is long, what a red tag obligates them to do, or how fast they have to act.
Explaining it plainly is unusually valuable and almost nobody does it.

Good topics: what an inspector actually checks, why the report has so many line
items, what happens between finding and repair, what the AHJ sees, what a
five-year internal obstruction investigation involves and why it costs what it
costs.

## Ask (10%)

One in ten posts, at most. Specific and plain. "We're scheduling annual sprinkler
inspections for Q2 in New Castle County. If your due date is coming up, call me."

No urgency manufacturing. No "limited availability." `shawn-voice` rules apply
with full force here, and this is the pillar where drafts most often slip into
sales language.

## Pillar selection

Given a job, ask which pillar it serves best. Most jobs can serve two. Do not
force a single job into all four.
