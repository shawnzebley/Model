# Formats and cadence

## Platform

LinkedIn, primarily. Shawn's buyers are property managers and facility directors,
and they are on LinkedIn and effectively nowhere else professionally.

Personal profile, not the company page. Personal profiles substantially outperform
company pages, and in this business the relationship is with Shawn anyway.

Ignore TikTok, Instagram, and Threads unless he raises them. The generic marketing
skills push these hard and they are wrong for this audience.

## LinkedIn text post

The default. 150 to 400 words.

- First two lines are the hook, visible before the fold
- Short paragraphs, one to three lines each
- White space between them, this audience reads on a phone
- No hashtag walls. Three at most, or none
- Links go in the first comment, not the post body
- One ask, or none

## LinkedIn carousel (PDF document post)

For anything sequential: what happens during an inspection, how a deficiency moves
from finding to repair, what the five-year internal obstruction investigation
involves.

Six to ten slides. One idea per slide. Large type. Slide one is the hook, last
slide is the ask or the plain close.

Build these with the `pptx` skill or as an artifact, then export to PDF. Canva and
Adobe are both connected if the piece needs real design.

## Follow-up email

Not social, but the same raw material feeds it. When a post performs, the same
finding usually makes a good touch to a specific account. Route these through
`shawn-voice`, keep them to a phone screen, one ask.

## Cadence

Two to three posts a week is sustainable and sufficient. Five is not necessary
and the quality drops because the real material runs out.

Suggested week:
- One field proof
- One plain-English code
- One how-the-work-works or ask, alternating

## Batching

Batch from job activity, not from a blank page. Pull two to four weeks of
completed work, extract every atom, sort by pillar, then draft.

Say plainly if the material only supports four posts when he asked for eight.
Fewer real posts beat padded ones, and padding is how the account starts sounding
like every other vendor.

## Repurposing one job

A single quarterly inspection with a real finding typically yields:

1. Field proof post on the finding
2. Code explainer on why that component is inspected at that frequency
3. How-it-works post on what else gets checked in the same visit
4. A carousel if the finding has a good sequence to it

Space them out. Do not run all four in one week from one job.
