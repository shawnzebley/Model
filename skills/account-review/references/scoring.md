# Scoring and risk

## Expansion scoring

Rate each opportunity 1 to 10 on three dimensions.

**Value.** Revenue if it closes. Use bands, not invented precision. Anything that
needs a real number goes through `fire-inspection-quote`.

**Effort.** What it takes to land and deliver. A five-year internal obstruction
investigation on an account that already trusts Sobieski is low effort. A panel
replacement requiring a survey, a proposal, and a capital approval is high.

**Likelihood.** Honest probability. Anchor it to something real:

- Work the code requires and the customer has no record of: high
- Open deficiency already quoted, customer went quiet: medium
- Open deficiency the customer explicitly declined: low, and note why
- Net new service on a happy account: medium
- Capital project with no budget conversation yet: low

Composite: value plus likelihood, weighted, minus effort. Rank on the composite.

Tier the results:

- **Now.** High composite, and either urgent or time-bound by a due date.
- **This quarter.** Real, no forcing function yet.
- **Watch.** Genuine but not actionable. Missing budget, missing decision-maker,
  or waiting on a cycle date.

Do not pad the Now tier. Three real items Shawn will act on beat twelve he will
not.

## Retention risk

Recurring inspection accounts do not churn loudly. They churn at renewal, quietly,
usually to a lower bid, and usually after months of no contact.

Score these:

**Contact gap.** Months since a real conversation, not an invoice or an automated
scheduling email. This is the strongest single signal.

**Deficiency backlog.** Counterintuitive but reliable. A large aging backlog often
means the customer stopped engaging, and a competitor offering to "start clean"
lands easily against it.

**Service history.** Missed appointments, reschedules, a callback, a billing
dispute. One bad experience on a low-touch account outweighs years of clean work
because there is nothing else in recent memory.

**Price sensitivity.** Prior pushback, a bid request, a customer who mentioned
shopping it.

**Contact change.** New property manager with no relationship to Shawn.

**Renewal proximity.** Inside 120 days, everything above weighs more.

Status: Stable, Watch, or At Risk.

An At Risk account with expansion opportunity is not an expansion account. Fix
the relationship first. Pitching into a bad account accelerates the loss.

## Confidence

State it. If the review ran on partial data, say which pieces were missing and
how that limits the conclusions.

A review that reads confident on thin data is the failure mode to avoid. Same
discipline as `fire-inspection-quote`: no plausible-looking numbers filling gaps.
